@topic 1.1 | Contextualizing Period 1
- In 1491 the Americas were home to millions of people living in diverse, complex societies shaped by their environments. They had no contact with Europe or Africa.
- Changes in Europe set up exploration: stronger **centralized monarchies** (Spain completed its unification in 1492), demand for **Asian trade goods** and a sea route to reach them, the **Renaissance** and new maritime technology, and religious rivalry sharpened by the **Protestant Reformation** (1517).
- Portugal's voyages along the West African coast opened new trade, including a trade in enslaved people.
- **Columbus's voyage in 1492** began lasting contact among Europeans, Native Americans, and Africans. The period ends in 1607 with **Jamestown**, the first permanent English settlement.

% European monarchs and merchants wanted direct access to Asian spices and luxury goods. The Enlightenment and the Industrial Revolution came centuries later, and religious rivalry was growing, not declining.

@topic 1.2 | Native American Societies Before European Contact
- Native peoples adapted to and transformed their environments, which produced very different societies in different regions.
- **Maize cultivation** spread north from present-day Mexico. In the **Southwest** it supported settled communities, irrigation, and social diversification (Pueblo peoples).
- In the **Great Basin** and on the western **Great Plains**, dry land and scarce resources led to largely **mobile** lifestyles based on hunting and gathering.
- In the **Northeast**, the **Mississippi River Valley**, and along the **Atlantic seaboard**, societies mixed farming with hunting and gathering and built permanent villages. Examples: the Iroquois (Haudenosaunee) and the Mississippian city of Cahokia.
- In the **Northwest** and present-day **California**, peoples lived by hunting, gathering, and fishing; rich ocean resources supported settled communities such as those of the Chinook.

@topic 1.3 | European Exploration in the Americas
- European states explored for wealth, power, and religion: new sources of gold and trade, an edge over rival kingdoms, and converts to Christianity.
- **Portugal** led early, sailing around Africa to reach Asia. **Spain** backed Columbus's westward voyage in 1492.
- The **Treaty of Tordesillas** (1494) divided newly claimed lands between Spain and Portugal.
- New technology made long voyages possible: the caravel, the compass, the astrolabe, and better maps.
- France and England began exploring later, looking for a northwest passage to Asia.

@topic 1.4 | Columbian Exchange, Spanish Exploration, and Conquest
- The **Columbian Exchange** was the transfer of plants, animals, people, and diseases between the Eastern and Western Hemispheres after 1492.
- To the Americas: horses, cattle, pigs, wheat, sugar cane, and diseases such as **smallpox** and measles. Epidemics killed a large share of the Native population.
- To Europe, Africa, and Asia: **maize, potatoes**, tomatoes, cacao, and tobacco. The new food crops supported population growth.
- Gold and silver from the Americas and expanded trade helped Europe shift from **feudalism toward capitalism**.
- Spanish conquistadors toppled the **Aztec** Empire (Cortés, 1519–1521) and the **Inca** Empire (Pizarro, 1530s), helped by disease, steel weapons, horses, and Native allies.

*c) The spread of epidemic diseases such as smallpox
d) The export of maize and potatoes to Europe
% Native Americans had no immunity to European diseases. Epidemics caused a catastrophic population decline.

a) Horses and cattle
b) Wheat and sugar cane
c) Smallpox and measles
*d) Maize and potatoes
% Maize and potatoes were American crops that became staples in Europe. Horses, cattle, wheat, sugar cane, and the listed diseases traveled in the other direction.
? The Columbian Exchange contributed to which change in Europe?
a) A sharp decline in population
*b) Population growth and a shift from feudalism toward capitalism
c) The end of rivalry among European monarchies
d) A return to an economy based on barter
% New food crops improved nutrition and raised population, while American gold and silver and growing trade encouraged commercial, profit-seeking economies.

@topic 1.5 | Labor, Slavery, and Caste in the Spanish Colonial System
- Under the **encomienda system**, the Spanish crown granted colonists the right to demand labor and tribute from Native Americans in a given area, in return for supposedly protecting and Christianizing them. In practice it meant forced labor in fields and mines.
- As disease and overwork reduced the Native population, and as the crown tried to limit abuses (the **New Laws of 1542**), the Spanish turned increasingly to **enslaved Africans** for plantation and mining labor.
- The Spanish developed a **casta system** that ranked people by racial ancestry: Europeans born in Spain at the top, then American-born Spaniards, then people of mixed ancestry such as **mestizos** (Spanish and Native American), with Native Americans and Africans at the bottom.

d) seats in a colonial legislature
% The encomienda was a grant of Native labor and tribute, not of land. Land given for paying a servant's passage describes the English headright system.

c) Spanish settlers refused to live near Native Americans
d) Portugal required Spain to buy enslaved people under the Treaty of Tordesillas
% With the Native population collapsing, colonists looked to the Atlantic slave trade for workers on plantations and in mines.
? In the Spanish colonial casta system, a person's social status was based mainly on
a) wealth earned through trade
b) military rank
*c) racial ancestry
d) length of residence in the colony
% The casta system defined a hierarchy of categories based on a person's mix of European, Native American, and African ancestry.

@topic 1.6 | Cultural Interactions Between Europeans, Native Americans, and Africans
- Europeans and Native Americans held different views on religion, gender roles, family, **land use**, and power. Europeans treated land as private property; many Native societies treated it as a shared resource.
- Contact brought misunderstanding and conflict, and also exchange: each side adopted useful goods and knowledge from the other.
- Native peoples defended their sovereignty, economies, and beliefs through both **diplomacy** and **military resistance**.
- Spaniards debated how Native Americans should be treated. **Bartolomé de las Casas** condemned Spanish cruelty and argued that Native Americans were fully human. **Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda** argued that they were naturally inferior and that conquest was justified. The two argued their cases in the **Valladolid debate** (1550–1551).
- Europeans developed belief systems that justified the subjugation of Africans and Native Americans.

a) Native Americans were naturally suited to slavery
*b) Native Americans were fully human and Spanish abuses against them should end
c) Spain should abandon its American colonies entirely
d) Native Americans should be removed to make room for Spanish farmers
% Las Casas, a Dominican friar, documented Spanish cruelty and pressed the crown for reform. The view that Native Americans were natural slaves was Sepúlveda's.
? Which of the following was a major difference between European and Native American worldviews at the time of contact?
*a) Europeans generally viewed land as property that individuals could own and sell, while many Native societies viewed land as a shared resource
b) Europeans practiced organized religion, while Native Americans had no religious beliefs
c) Native Americans traded with other groups, while Europeans did not
d) Europeans lived in permanent settlements, while all Native societies were nomadic
% Different ideas about land ownership caused repeated conflict. The other choices are false generalizations: Native societies had religions, and many lived in permanent settlements.

@topic 1.7 | Causation in Period 1

- **Effects in Europe**: new crops and population growth, new wealth, the growth of capitalism, and sharper rivalry as other states sought their own colonies.

@topic 2.3 | The Regions of British Colonies
- **Chesapeake** (Virginia, Maryland) and North Carolina: **tobacco**, a labor-intensive cash crop. Workers were at first mostly male **indentured servants**, later **enslaved Africans**. The **House of Burgesses** (1619) was the first elected assembly in the English colonies. Maryland was founded as a refuge for Catholics.
- **New England**: settled by **Puritans** who came as families. Small towns, family farms, and a mixed economy of farming, fishing, shipbuilding, and trade. **Town meetings** gave local self-government. Dissenters such as **Roger Williams** and **Anne Hutchinson** were banished; Williams founded Rhode Island.
- **Middle colonies** (New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware): exported **grain**. The most ethnically and religiously **diverse** region. William Penn founded Pennsylvania as a Quaker colony that practiced religious toleration.
- **Southern Atlantic coast and British West Indies**: long growing seasons and plantations growing staple crops such as **rice** and **sugar**. Enslaved Africans were often the majority of the population.
- Distance and loose British oversight let colonies create self-governing institutions that were unusually democratic for the time. In the South, elite planters dominated the assemblies.

@topic 2.4 | Transatlantic Trade
- An **Atlantic economy** linked Europe, Africa, and the Americas through the exchange of goods, enslaved Africans and American Indians, and ideas. The pattern is often called the **triangular trade**.
- **Mercantilism** held that colonies exist to enrich the home country: they supply raw materials and buy its manufactured goods.
- The **Navigation Acts** (beginning in 1651) required trade to be carried on English or colonial ships and required certain colonial goods, such as tobacco and sugar, to be sent only to England.
- Enforcement was loose and smuggling was common. This period of light British oversight is called **salutary neglect**.
- Trade with Europeans brought new goods into American Indian communities and spread epidemic diseases.

a) develop their own manufacturing to compete with the home country
*b) supply raw materials to the home country and buy its manufactured goods
c) trade freely with all nations
d) serve as places to send political dissenters
% Mercantilists measured national power by wealth and a favorable balance of trade, and they saw colonies as a way to increase both.
? The Navigation Acts required that
a) colonists pay a direct tax on printed materials
*b) certain colonial products be shipped only to England and trade be carried on English or colonial ships
c) colonists house British soldiers in their homes
d) the colonies stop importing enslaved Africans
% The acts put mercantilism into law by steering colonial trade through England. A tax on printed materials describes the later Stamp Act.
? The British policy later called salutary neglect had which effect?
a) It ended colonial smuggling
*b) It let colonists grow used to governing themselves with little interference
c) It closed the colonial assemblies
d) It cut the colonies off from Atlantic trade
% Because Britain enforced its trade laws loosely for decades, colonists came to expect a large degree of self-rule.

@topic 2.5 | Interactions Between American Indians and Europeans

- British colonists fought American Indians over land, resources, and boundaries. In **Metacom's War** (King Philip's War, 1675–1676), the Wampanoag leader Metacom and his allies attacked New England towns; their defeat broke Native power in southern New England.
- In the **Pueblo Revolt** (1680), Pueblo peoples drove the Spanish out of New Mexico for more than a decade. After returning, the Spanish accommodated some aspects of Pueblo culture.
- Trade in furs and guns changed Native economies and intensified warfare among Native groups.

@topic 2.6 | Slavery in the British Colonies
- All British colonies took part in the **Atlantic slave trade**, driven by abundant land, European demand for colonial goods, and a shortage of indentured servants.
- New England held relatively few enslaved people. The Chesapeake and the southern Atlantic coast held many. The great majority of enslaved Africans were sent to the **West Indies**.
- **Bacon's Rebellion** (1676) showed Chesapeake planters the danger of a large class of discontented former servants and sped the shift to enslaved African labor.
- **Chattel slavery** treated people as property. Laws made slavery hereditary through the mother and created a strict racial system that banned interracial relationships.
- Enslaved people resisted **overtly**, as in the **Stono Rebellion** (South Carolina, 1739), and **covertly**, by working slowly, breaking tools, running away, and preserving family ties, culture, and religion.

d) The Navigation Acts

@topic 2.7 | Colonial Society and Culture
- The **First Great Awakening** (1730s–1740s) was a wave of Protestant revivals led by preachers such as **Jonathan Edwards** and **George Whitefield**. - The **Enlightenment** stressed reason and natural rights. **John Locke** argued that people have natural rights and that government rests on the consent of the governed. Benjamin Franklin embodied Enlightenment thinking in the colonies.
- **Anglicization**: the colonies became more like Britain in their political institutions, law, trade, print culture, and Protestant religion.
- The **Zenger trial** (1735) was an early victory for freedom of the press.
- Colonists' resistance to imperial control drew on their experience of self-government, ideas of liberty, the Enlightenment, religious independence, and a belief that the imperial system was corrupt.

@topic 3.1 | Contextualizing Period 3

- Colonists, used to governing themselves, resisted. Resistance grew into a war for independence and then into an experiment in republican government.
- The new nation argued over how strong its central government should be, how to deal with Britain and France, and what to do about slavery and American Indian land.
- **Dates to anchor the period**: 1754 French and Indian War begins; 1763 Treaty of Paris and Proclamation Line; 1765 Stamp Act; 1776 Declaration of Independence; 1781 Yorktown and Articles of Confederation; 1787 Constitutional Convention; 1789 Washington inaugurated; 1800 Jefferson elected.

@topic 3.2 | The Seven Years' War (The French and Indian War)
- Britain and France fought for control of the Ohio River Valley. Most American Indian nations sided with the French, who were traders and not land-hungry settlers.
- At the **Albany Congress** (1754), Benjamin Franklin proposed the **Albany Plan of Union** for a common colonial defense. The colonies rejected it.
- The **Treaty of Paris of 1763** removed France from North America. Britain gained Canada and all land east of the Mississippi River.
- **Pontiac's Rebellion** (1763) led Britain to issue the **Proclamation of 1763**, which barred colonial settlement west of the Appalachian Mountains. Colonists resented it.
- The war doubled Britain's debt. Parliament decided the colonies should help pay for their own defense.
? The Proclamation of 1763 angered many colonists because it
a) required them to house British soldiers
*b) prohibited settlement west of the Appalachian Mountains

@topic 4.12 | African Americans in the Early Republic

- Enslaved people preserved families, religion, and culture, and resisted through work slowdowns, escape, and revolt.
- **Nat Turner's Rebellion** (Virginia, 1831) killed about sixty white people. Southern states answered with harsher slave codes and limits on teaching enslaved people to read.

@topic 4.13 | The Society of the South in the Early Republic

- After the international slave trade ended in 1808, a large **internal slave trade** moved enslaved people from the upper South to the cotton states and broke up families.
- Most white southerners owned no slaves, but a small class of wealthy planters dominated society and politics.
- Southern leaders such as John C. Calhoun came to defend slavery as a **"positive good"** and not merely a necessary evil.
- The South had few cities, factories, or immigrants compared with the North.

- Compromises failed during the 1850s, and the election of Abraham Lincoln led to secession and civil war.

@topic 5.2 | Manifest Destiny
- **Manifest Destiny** was the belief that the United States was meant to spread across the continent, bringing its institutions with it.
- Settlers followed the Oregon Trail to the Pacific Northwest. The United States and Britain divided the Oregon Country at the 49th parallel in 1846.
- **Texas**, which had won independence from Mexico in 1836, was annexed in 1845.
- The **California Gold Rush** (1849) drew a flood of migrants. Expansion brought conflict with American Indians and Mexicans already living in the West.

@topic 5.3 | The Mexican–American War
- President **James K. Polk** wanted California and the Southwest. A clash in disputed territory along the Rio Grande led to war in 1846.
- In the **Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo** (1848), Mexico gave up California and most of the present-day Southwest, the **Mexican Cession**, for \$15 million.
- The **Wilmot Proviso** proposed banning slavery in any land taken from Mexico. It passed the House but not the Senate and inflamed sectional feeling.
- Mexicans and American Indians in the new territory were promised rights, but many lost land and political power.

@topic 5.4 | The Compromise of 1850
- California's request to enter as a free state threatened the balance between free and slave states.
- The **Compromise of 1850** admitted California as a free state, let the Utah and New Mexico territories decide on slavery by **popular sovereignty**, ended the slave trade in Washington, D.C., and passed a stronger **Fugitive Slave Act**.
- The Fugitive Slave Act required northerners to help return people who had escaped slavery. It angered the North and strengthened the antislavery movement.

% The law forced northerners to take part in capturing people who had escaped and denied the accused a jury trial.

c) the Supreme Court would decide whether a territory allowed slavery

@topic 5.5 | Sectional Conflict: Regional Differences

- Abolitionists were a minority, but **_Uncle Tom's Cabin_** (1852) by Harriet Beecher Stowe turned many northerners against slavery. The **Underground Railroad** helped people escape.
- The **South** remained agricultural and dependent on enslaved labor, and its leaders defended slavery and states' rights.

@topic 5.6 | Failure of Compromise

- In **_Dred Scott v. Sandford_** (1857), the Supreme Court ruled that Black Americans were not citizens and that Congress could not ban slavery in the territories.
- In the **Lincoln–Douglas debates** (1858), Lincoln argued against the spread of slavery. **John Brown's raid** on Harpers Ferry (1859) terrified the South.

@topic 5.7 | Election of 1860 and Secession

- **South Carolina** seceded in December 1860, and other Deep South states followed. They formed the **Confederate States of America**.
- The war began when Confederate forces fired on **Fort Sumter** in April 1861. Four more states then seceded. The slaveholding **border states** stayed in the Union.

@topic 5.9 | Government Policies During the Civil War

- In the **Gettysburg Address**, Lincoln described the war as a struggle to preserve a nation dedicated to equality.
- With southerners gone from Congress, Republicans passed the **Homestead Act**, the Pacific Railway Act, and the Morrill Land-Grant Act (all 1862).
- Lincoln suspended habeas corpus in some areas, and both sides drafted soldiers. The **Thirteenth Amendment** (1865) abolished slavery.

@topic 5.10 | Reconstruction
- **Reconstruction** (1865–1877) dealt with how to readmit the southern states and what rights formerly enslaved people would have.
- The **Thirteenth Amendment** abolished slavery. The **Fourteenth** granted citizenship and equal protection of the laws. The **Fifteenth** said the vote could not be denied because of race.
- President **Andrew Johnson** was lenient toward the South. Southern states passed **Black Codes** that restricted freedpeople.
- **Radical Republicans** in Congress took control, placed the South under military rule, and required new state constitutions. The House **impeached** Johnson, and the Senate fell one vote short of removing him.
- The **Freedmen's Bureau** provided food, schools, and legal help. Black men voted and held office.
- The women's rights movement split over the Fifteenth Amendment because it did not include women.

a) abolished slavery

d) ended military rule in the South
% It overturned the _Dred Scott_ ruling on citizenship and became the basis for later civil rights decisions.

a) guarantee voting rights for freedmen
*b) restrict the freedom of African Americans and keep them as a dependent labor force
c) distribute land to formerly enslaved people
d) carry out the Fourteenth Amendment
% The codes limited where freedpeople could work, travel, and live. They convinced Congress that stronger federal action was needed.
? Radical Republicans in Congress differed from President Andrew Johnson because they wanted to
a) readmit the southern states immediately with no conditions
*b) protect the rights of freedpeople and require changes in the South before readmission
c) pay slaveholders for their losses
d) end the Freedmen's Bureau
% Johnson pardoned former Confederates and vetoed civil rights measures. Congress overrode him and imposed its own plan.

@topic 5.11 | Failure of Reconstruction
- Most freedpeople got no land. Many became **sharecroppers**, farming a planter's land for a share of the crop and falling into debt.
- White supremacist groups such as the **Ku Klux Klan** used violence to keep Black southerners from voting.
- Northern support faded because of economic depression, corruption scandals, and weariness.
- In the **Compromise of 1877**, Republican Rutherford B. Hayes became president after a disputed election, and federal troops left the South. Reconstruction ended.
- Southern governments then stripped Black citizens of rights, but the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendments remained as a basis for later civil rights victories.
