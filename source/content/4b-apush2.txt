- The **Homestead Act** drew farmers to the Great Plains. Mining and cattle ranching boomed.
- New machinery raised farm output, but crop prices fell. Farmers faced high railroad rates and debt.
- Farmers organized in the **Grange** and the **Farmers' Alliances** to demand regulation of railroads and cheaper credit.

- The **Dawes Act** (1887) broke up reservation land into individual family plots to push American Indians to live as farmers. Much of the land ended up in white hands.
- Boarding schools tried to erase Native languages and cultures. The army's killing of Lakota at **Wounded Knee** (1890) marked the end of armed resistance.
- Many American Indians preserved their cultures and identities despite these policies.

- African Americans began leaving the South. Some, called Exodusters, moved to Kansas.

- **Settlement houses**, such as **Jane Addams's Hull House** in Chicago, offered immigrants classes, child care, and other help.
- Many reformers pushed immigrants to adopt American customs. Immigrants also kept their own languages, religions, and traditions.

@topic 6.10 | Development of the Middle Class

- With more income and free time, people spent on **leisure**: spectator sports, vaudeville, amusement parks, and department stores.
- Wealthy donors, following the Gospel of Wealth, funded libraries, universities, and museums.

- The census of 1890 reported that the frontier had closed, which made some look abroad.
- **Anti-imperialists**, including Mark Twain and William Jennings Bryan, argued that ruling other peoples without their consent violated American principles of self-government.

- Filipinos who wanted independence fought a long, bloody war against American rule.
- The **Open Door policy** sought equal trading rights in China. The **Roosevelt Corollary** claimed a right to police Latin America, and the United States built the **Panama Canal**.

- **Theodore Roosevelt** broke up trusts, regulated business, and conserved natural resources. **Woodrow Wilson** created the **Federal Reserve** and strengthened antitrust law.

@topic 7.9 | The Great Depression
- The **stock market crash** of October 1929 began the Depression. Deeper causes were overproduction, unequal distribution of wealth, risky borrowing and speculation, weak banks, and high tariffs.
- By 1933 about a **quarter** of workers were unemployed. Banks failed, and farms and homes were lost.
- Drought turned the southern plains into the **Dust Bowl**, and many farm families left for California.
- President **Herbert Hoover** believed in limited government and voluntary action. His response was seen as too little.

*b) Overproduction of goods and an unequal distribution of income that limited what consumers could buy
c) A shortage of factory capacity
d) High wages for farm workers
% Factories and farms produced more than people could afford to buy, and much of the decade's prosperity rested on credit.

*b) relied on voluntary action and opposed large-scale direct federal relief
c) closed all of the banks
d) raised income taxes on the poor
% Hoover feared that direct federal aid would weaken self-reliance. As conditions worsened, voters turned against him.

@topic 7.10 | The New Deal
- **Franklin D. Roosevelt** took office in 1933 and promised relief for the needy, recovery for the economy, and reform of the financial system.
- Programs created jobs (**Civilian Conservation Corps**, **Works Progress Administration**), insured bank deposits (**FDIC**), regulated the stock market (**SEC**), and paid farmers to cut production.
- The **Social Security Act** (1935) created pensions for the elderly and unemployment insurance. The **Wagner Act** protected workers' right to form unions.
- Critics on the right said the New Deal gave government too much power. Critics on the left, such as Huey Long, said it did too little. Roosevelt's plan to add justices to the Supreme Court failed.
- The New Deal did not end the Depression, but it greatly **expanded the federal government's role** and drew African Americans, union members, and ethnic voters into the Democratic Party.
? The Social Security Act of 1935 was significant because it
a) ended unemployment
*b) made the federal government responsible for providing old-age pensions and unemployment insurance
