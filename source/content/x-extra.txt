@topic 2.1
? A farmer sells wheat to a miller for \$1. The miller sells flour to a baker for \$3. The baker sells bread to a consumer for \$6. How much do these transactions add to GDP?
a) \$3
*b) \$6
c) \$9
d) \$10
% Only the final good counts, so GDP rises by \$6. The value added at each stage ($1 + 2 + 3$) gives the same total. Adding every sale (\$10) double counts the wheat and flour.

@topic 2.3
? A country has an adult population of 250 million, a labor force participation rate of 60%, and an unemployment rate of 6%. How many people are unemployed?
a) 6 million
*b) 9 million
c) 15 million
d) 141 million
% The labor force is $0.60 \times 250 = 150$ million, and $0.06 \times 150 = 9$ million are unemployed. Taking 6% of the whole population gives 15 million, which is wrong.

@topic 2.4

*a) \$20.00
b) \$22.00
c) \$24.20
d) \$32.00
% Real wage $= \frac{22}{110} \times 100 = 20$. Multiplying by 1.10 instead of dividing gives \$24.20.

@topic 2.6
? Real GDP is \$500 billion and the GDP deflator is 120. What is nominal GDP?
a) \$417 billion
b) \$500 billion
*c) \$600 billion
d) \$620 billion
% Nominal GDP $= \text{real GDP} \times \frac{\text{deflator}}{100} = 500 \times 1.20 = 600$. Dividing by 1.20 gives \$417 billion, which goes the wrong way.

@topic 3.2
? When disposable income rises from \$400 billion to \$500 billion, consumption rises from \$350 billion to \$430 billion. What is the expenditure multiplier?
a) 1.25
b) 4
*c) 5
d) 8
% $\text{MPC} = \frac{430 - 350}{500 - 400} = 0.8$, so the multiplier is $\frac{1}{1 - 0.8} = 5$. The value 1.25 is $1/\text{MPC}$, a common slip.

@topic 3.5
? An economy's unemployment rate is 3% and its natural rate of unemployment is 5%. The economy has
a) a recessionary gap, with real GDP below potential output
*b) an inflationary gap, with real GDP above potential output
c) no output gap, because unemployment is low
d) a recessionary gap, with real GDP above potential output
% Unemployment below the natural rate means the economy is producing more than its full-employment output, which is a positive (inflationary) gap.

@topic 3.7
? An economy in long-run equilibrium is hit by a sharp rise in oil prices, and the government takes no action. In the long run,
a) aggregate demand shifts right until full employment returns at a higher price level
*b) high unemployment lowers nominal wages, and short-run aggregate supply shifts back to the right until output returns to potential
c) long-run aggregate supply shifts right to offset the higher costs
d) the economy stays permanently below potential output
% The supply shock creates a recessionary gap. Over time, falling nominal wages lower production costs, so SRAS shifts back and output returns to potential.

@topic 3.8
? An economy has an inflationary gap of \$120 billion and a marginal propensity to consume of 0.8. By how much should taxes be raised to close the gap?
a) \$24 billion
*b) \$30 billion
c) \$96 billion
d) \$120 billion
% The tax multiplier has size $\frac{0.8}{0.2} = 4$, and $120/4 = 30$. The \$24 billion answer is the needed cut in government purchases, which uses the expenditure multiplier of 5.

@topic 2.4

@topic 2.7

@topic 2.8
